# TMLR Rebuttal Draft

We thank the reviewer for the constructive feedback. We agree that the paper should be clearer for a broad ML audience and that the evaluation claims need stronger uncertainty analysis. We address the main points below and will revise the manuscript accordingly.

1. **Uncertainty, heterogeneous averages, and claim scope.** We agree that the `Avg.` column should not be the sole basis for interpretation because it combines accuracy, exact-match scores, F1 variants, and normalized CVSS scoring. We located the row-level Eval12 outputs and computed paired bootstrap intervals over evaluation examples, with point estimates verified against the saved LLMBench summaries. The 12-column average MinervaRL-GRPO deltas are +4.2 pp [3.5, 5.1] for Llama-8B, +6.2 pp [5.3, 7.2] for Llama-3B, +6.4 pp [5.6, 7.7] for Qwen-8B, and +0.4 pp [-0.8, 1.3] for Qwen-4B. Thus, the average improvement is clearly separated from GRPO for three of four backbones, while Qwen-4B should be described more cautiously. We will add per-task denominators, paired bootstrap intervals, and task-family averages, and we will revise language such as "consistent gains" where task-level results are mixed. In particular, taxonomy-mapping tasks improve across all four backbones (+9.6, +13.4, +7.2, and +3.2 pp; all paired CIs exclude zero), while choice/reasoning tasks are mixed.

2. **Rollout-sensitive evaluation.** The reviewer is right that final pass@k or best-of-k evaluation would directly test the support-expansion mechanism. The current paper provides training-time evidence through the zero-solve fraction in Figure 3: MinervaRL reduces all-zero rollout groups relative to GRPO across all four backbones, and for Llama-8B, increasing GRPO from 8 to 12 rollouts still does not close the gap. Table 4 also shows that GRPO with 12 rollouts (Avg. 51.8) does not match MinervaRL (Avg. 63.3) on validation. We will add held-out rollout-aware metrics under matched sampling budgets. For exact-match and taxonomy tasks, we will report pass@1/pass@k or verifier success@k; for graded, multi-label, and extraction tasks, we will report best-of-k verifier score/F1 or thresholded success@k. We will also explicitly relate these metrics to the zero-solve analysis.

3. **Component attribution and ablations.** We agree that the ablation discussion should make the role of each component clearer. Table 4(a) already separates several alternatives in the Llama-8B validation setting: GRPO is 53.8 Avg., GRPO with 12 rollouts is 51.8, answer-only SFT is 48.7, full MinervaRL is 63.3, no EMA teacher is 62.6, no filtering is 62.2, and no ML filter is 61.6. We will revise the text to make the interpretation explicit: no ACR corresponds to the GRPO control; answer-only SFT tests whether added supervised compute alone explains the gain; no filtering removes both heuristic and TextCNN filtering; no ML filter keeps heuristic filtering but removes the learned quality filter. These results indicate that the main improvement is not recovered by more rollouts or answer-only SFT, and that the filtering/teacher components contribute smaller but measurable validation gains. Where compute permits, we will add final Eval12 ablations for the main variants; otherwise we will clearly label Table 4 as validation-side evidence rather than a full 12-task attribution study.

4. **Clarity, notation, and CTI accessibility.** We agree that the introduction, Figure 1, task descriptions, and Section 4.1 notation currently assume too much CTI background. We will revise the introduction around a concrete example: mapping an unstructured vulnerability or threat report into a machine-readable CTI label such as a CWE, ATT&CK technique, CVSS vector, or mitigation set. We will simplify Figure 1 by separating the standard GRPO loop from the auxiliary ACR/SFT loop, rather than presenting a long 15-step pipeline. We will also add a compact task/metric table before Table 1, grouping benchmarks into multiple-choice/reasoning, taxonomy mapping, multi-label mitigation prediction, CVSS scoring, and information extraction. In Section 4.1 and Algorithm 1, we will unify notation for the verifier reward, distinguish original prompts from answer-conditioned prompts, and rewrite dataset notation as paired examples rather than ambiguous singleton sets.

5. **Theory framing.** We agree that Appendix H should not be presented as a theory of full language-model optimization. Its useful role is narrower: it formalizes the finite-sampling intuition that, when the current policy has low probability of sampling a verifier-correct answer under a small rollout budget, GRPO may repeatedly observe all-zero groups. The theorem as written relies on assumptions about accepted ACR traces and distillation improving original-prompt success probability, so it should be framed as a toy support-seeding model rather than a proof of the complete algorithm. We will move or shorten this material, state the assumptions more plainly, and avoid implying that it analyzes GRPO/SFT interaction, gradient interference, partial-credit rewards, or full LM dynamics.

6. **Broader impact and mitigation.** We will expand the broader-impact statement with concrete mitigations. The intended use is defensive CTI analysis: triage, vulnerability mapping, mitigation recommendation, and report structuring. Before release of derived artifacts or checkpoints, we will document defensive-use constraints, run cyber-safety red-teaming, avoid releasing data that materially facilitates offensive automation beyond already-public benchmarks, and provide model-card guidance that discourages malicious use and describes known limitations.

Overall, we will revise the paper to make the contribution easier to understand, replace unsupported aggregate wording with uncertainty-aware claims, and distinguish what is established by the current experiments from what remains a limitation.
